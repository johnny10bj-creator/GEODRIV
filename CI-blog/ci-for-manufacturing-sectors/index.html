CI for Manufacturing Sectors

Aerospace, medical devices, automotive, and industrial automation each demand a different competitive intelligence approach. One size does not fit all.

The one-sentence version: The signal types that matter vary dramatically by manufacturing sector. Aerospace CI centres on certifications (AS9100, NADCAP). Medical device CI tracks FDA 510(k) clearances and patent clusters. Automotive CI follows EV transition signals. Industrial automation CI focuses on platform ecosystems and M&A. A generic CI tool captures none of these well.

Aerospace & Medical Devices: The Certification-Driven Sectors

In these sectors, a new certification is a product roadmap.

Aerospace & Defence

CI in aerospace revolves around certification and qualification. The key question isn't "what do they sell?" but "what are they qualified to build?" Track AS9100, NADCAP certifications, and prime supplier list additions. New certification = new capability = new competitive threat, often 6-12 months before any product announcement.

Medical Devices

Regulatory filings are public — and they're predictive. FDA 510(k) clearances, CE MDR certificates, and clinical trial registrations provide a 12-24 month forward look into competitor product pipelines. Cross-reference clearance dates with patent clusters to identify which technology platforms competitors are commercialising.

Key insight: In certification-driven sectors, the regulatory database is your most powerful CI tool — if you know which fields to monitor and how to interpret clearance patterns as competitive signals.

Automotive & Industrial Automation: The Ecosystem Shift

Watch for cross-sector entrants and platform pivots.

Automotive Supply Chain

The EV transition is reshaping the supply chain. CI signals to track: battery chemistry investments, gigacasting capability announcements, IATF 16949 certification expansions, and OEM supply awards. Your most dangerous competitor may not be a traditional tier-1 supplier — it could be a battery manufacturer moving upstream.

Industrial Automation

Here, CI is about ecosystem intelligence. Who is partnering with whom? Which platforms are gaining traction? M&A activity reveals strategic intent — a sensor company acquiring a software startup is building an IIoT platform, not just buying technology.

The question isn't "who competes with me today?" — it's "who will compete with me in 18 months from a completely different industry?" — GEODRIV Manufacturing CI Framework
