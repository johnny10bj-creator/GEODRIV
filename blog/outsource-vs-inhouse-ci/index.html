The Real Cost of Building an In-House CI Team

Most mid-market manufacturers spend $200K+ on an internal CI function that delivers less than a $3K/month outsourced system. Here's the math.

The short version: hiring one CI analyst costs $120-180K/year fully loaded — and they still need tools, data sources, and methodology. An outsourced CI system, purpose-built for your industry, starts at $3K/month with everything included. But cost isn't the real difference.

The In-House Math

Let's price a functional one-person CI function in manufacturing:

Line itemAnnual cost
Senior CI analyst (salary + benefits)$120,000 – $180,000
CI software subscription (Crayon/Klue tier)$15,000 – $30,000
Premium data sources (patent DB, tender platforms, news aggregation)$8,000 – $25,000
Training, conferences, methodology development$5,000 – $10,000
Total year one$148,000 – $245,000

And that's for one person. One person who gets sick, takes vacation, and has a finite attention span for tracking 30+ competitors across four dimensions.

Meanwhile, the same spend buys you:

Domain expertiseOne hire's experienceCross-industry pattern library (15+ years)

When In-House Makes Sense

I'm not arguing against in-house CI. I've built in-house functions. They make sense when:

  • Your CI needs are so specific that no external framework applies (rare — most manufacturing CI follows predictable patterns)
  • You already have 2+ analysts and need a department head to coordinate (you're at scale)
  • CI is core to your product, not just a decision-support function (you sell CI as a service)

For everyone else — the mid-market manufacturer with 200-2,000 employees, competing in 2-5 segments, tracking 20-40 competitors — outsourced CI delivers more intelligence for less money.

The Hidden Cost

The biggest cost of in-house CI isn't the salary. It's the 12-month methodology ramp. While your analyst is figuring out which signals matter in your industry, your competitors are moving. An outsourced system deploys a battle-tested methodology in weeks — because the signal model already exists.

Cost is easy to compare. Time-to-intelligence is what compounds.
